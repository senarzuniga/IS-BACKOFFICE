1. Executive Summary

This document presents INGECART’s final commercial and operational proposal for the relocation and restart of the PSC Visalia corrugator line. The proposal has been structured to balance three priorities: time-to-market, technical reliability at start-up, and a realistic automation roadmap for future productivity gains.

After reviewing the relocation scope, confirming the actual source equipment base, reassessing site execution needs, and consolidating the local cost framework required at destination, the project has been reorganized into a phased structure. This structure separates the core relocation and start-up scope from the future productivity and intralogistics enhancement phases.

Under this approach, the immediate priority is to deliver a plant capable of reaching production as early and as safely as possible. Additional modules such as TRIPLEX process equipment and AMR intralogistics for WIP handling and internal material flow remain highly valuable, but can be deployed in a second stage with lower interference on production, once the relocated corrugator line is fully stabilized.

INGECART recommendation to PSC:

Proceed with Scenario B: the base relocation project plus INGETRANS automated reel logistics. This is the best balance between fast start-up, improved corrugator continuity, safer reel feeding, reduced forklift dependency, and a strong foundation for future plant automation.

TRIPLEX and AMR intralogistics remain recommended future phases because they reinforce long-term efficiency, but they are not strictly required to achieve the first industrial milestone: a fast and stable return to production capacity.

2. Why the Project Value Has Been Updated

The final project values differ from the early working versions of the offer because the project definition has matured as technical and commercial assumptions were validated. This is not a change in commercial intent; it is the result of a more accurate understanding of the real execution model required to relocate, install and restart the asset under PSC conditions.

2.1 Confirmation of the actual relocation baseline

Initial internal references were partly benchmarked against a previous BHS relocation package executed in Spain. During the latest review phase, it was confirmed that the correct relocation baseline for this proposal is the original machine configuration located in Turkey. That confirmation affects the effective relocation scope, logistics configuration, auxiliary services and the real site effort required to deliver the project.

2.2 Inclusion of on-site project leadership as a dedicated cost item

The final offer explicitly includes 8 months of On-Site Project Management (PMOS) within the machine relocation package. This resource is critical to coordinate dismantling interfaces, transport arrival sequencing, installation fronts, local contractors, start-up readiness and risk control during execution. It has therefore been separated and valued as a visible industrial delivery component rather than treated as an implicit overhead.

2.3 Consolidation of local destination costs and import exposure

The PSC local cost block has also been updated to reflect the destination reality of the project, including boiler, civil work, local transport, packaging support, crane rental and import-related fees. In particular, import duties and customs-related fees have been recalculated based on the project architecture and on the proportion of equipment value assumed to be subject to customs release.

2.4 Separation between “time-to-market” scope and future optimization scope

The proposal now clearly distinguishes between:

  • Immediate start-up scope: relocation, installation, commissioning and the equipment required to place the plant back into production as quickly as possible.
  • Future optimization scope: TRIPLEX process enhancements and AMR intralogistics, which are highly relevant for long-term efficiency but can be implemented later with lower disruption once the plant is operational.

Executive reading of the change:

The updated commercial structure should be understood as the result of project definition maturity. As the origin machine, destination execution model, site management needs, and customs exposure were clarified, the proposal was refined to reflect the real industrial effort required for a successful relocation and start-up.

6. Why Scenario B Is the Strongest First Step

The relocation project is not only a mechanical transfer of equipment. It is the re-creation of an industrial flow under new physical, operational and economic conditions. For that reason, the best first investment is not necessarily the one with the highest total automation content, but the one that most effectively protects the critical path to production.

6.1 INGETRANS acts directly on the corrugator’s most critical logistics dependency

Corrugator productivity is highly sensitive to reel availability, delivery timing, feeding discipline and the elimination of avoidable waiting. INGETRANS addresses exactly that bottleneck by automating the reel transport flow between storage / staging and the corrugator feeding positions. This is why it has a stronger immediate time-to-market logic than other optional packages.

6.2 It improves start-up stability without overloading the first implementation phase

TRIPLEX and AMR are both valuable, but they expand the transformation perimeter into additional process and intralogistics domains. Scenario B keeps the first project wave centered on the core production heartbeat: corrugator availability, feeding continuity and logistics safety around the line.

6.3 It creates the right backbone for future phases

Once the plant is running, TRIPLEX and AMR can be integrated in a more controlled manner with lower disruption. In this roadmap, INGETRANS is not only a standalone productivity enabler; it also acts as the first structured automation layer that supports the plant’s future digital and intralogistics evolution.

8. Future Phase Logic – TRIPLEX and AMR

INGECART does not recommend discarding TRIPLEX or AMR. On the contrary, both remain strategically relevant. The recommendation is to sequence them after the first industrial milestone so that PSC secures early production, stabilizes the relocated line, and then expands the plant’s performance envelope with lower implementation risk.

8.1 Scenario C – TRIPLEX as a future performance phase

TRIPLEX should be considered as a future process enhancement phase that can strengthen machine-side capability and operating performance once the relocated line is fully operational. It is a valid next step for deeper process optimization, but it is not the first block INGECART would prioritize ahead of reel logistics continuity.

8.2 Scenario D – AMR as a future intralogistics backbone

AMR intralogistics should be understood as the long-term orchestrator of internal flow efficiency. It is especially relevant for WIP movement, internal routing discipline and the gradual digitalization of material movement across the plant. Deployed in a second phase, it can be integrated with lower disruption and with better operational visibility of the plant’s actual post-relocation needs.

8.3 AMR optional waste-flow extension

In addition to WIP logistics, the same AMR fleet architecture can optionally be extended to manage internal scrap and waste flow from corrugator and converting workstations. By using metallic cages or dedicated mobile containers handled by AMRs, the plant could centralize scrap collection, weighing, traceability and repositioning with minimal manual intervention. This optional layer is not valued in the current scenario totals, but it is operationally coherent with the AMR roadmap.

9. Maintenance and Operating Cost Considerations in the Automated Scenarios

One of the relevant structural benefits of the automated scenarios—particularly Scenario B with INGETRANS, and later Scenario D with AMR intralogistics—is the reduction of maintenance exposure associated with conventional logistics assets.

In a traditional corrugator supply environment, a significant share of maintenance effort is consumed by:

  • Forklift fleet usage, wear, battery charging logistics and operator-driven handling stress.
  • Conveyor sections, transfer devices and other movement interfaces with high intervention frequency.
  • Unplanned damage or alignment issues caused by repeated mechanical handling around the line.

By replacing a large part of the reel-handling dependency on forklifts and simplifying the logistics architecture around the corrugator, automated logistics reduces the amount of equipment subject to repetitive manual use and high-frequency maintenance. The exact savings depend on PSC’s current maintenance structure, spare-part policy, operating hours and labour rates, but the direction of impact is clear: less forklift dependency and fewer conventional logistics interfaces translate into a lower maintenance burden and lower operating volatility.

10. Commercial Positioning Message to PSC

INGECART recommendation to PSC management

The project should be approached as a staged industrial transformation rather than as a single all-inclusive capex event. The first objective is to put the Visalia plant back into production quickly, with the highest possible start-up reliability. For that reason, INGECART recommends prioritizing the relocation platform and protecting the corrugator with automated reel logistics through INGETRANS.

Once the line is operational and stabilized, PSC can execute the next phases—TRIPLEX and AMR intralogistics—with lower production risk and with a clearer understanding of the plant’s real post-start-up bottlenecks. This approach preserves time-to-market while keeping the full automation roadmap open.
