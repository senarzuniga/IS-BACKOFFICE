PSC VISALIA EXECUTIVE BUSINESS CASE

BHS Corrugator Relocation as a time-to-market core project, with future expansion options through Triplex capability and AMR-driven intralogistics automation.

1. Executive Summary

Executive Summary

This document presents INGECART’s executive business case for the incorporation of corrugating capacity at PSC Visalia through the relocation and commissioning of a BHS corrugator line, together with the supporting plant infrastructure required to bring the operation into production as fast as possible and under a robust industrial execution model.

The project should not be read only as a machinery purchase. It is a strategic manufacturing decision with direct impact on PSC’s internal board supply, lead time control, operational autonomy, cost structure, and long-term plant scalability. For that reason, the proposal has been structured around three decision scenarios:

  • Scenario A — Time-to-Market Core Case: BHS corrugator relocation + plant peripherals + PSC local implementation items.
  • Scenario B — Future Expansion with Triplex: Scenario A plus Triplex capability as a second strategic layer for product range expansion and commercial flexibility.
  • Scenario C — Future Expansion with AMR Intralogistics: Scenario A plus AMR-based intralogistics for WIP orchestration, material traceability and waste-control architecture.

INGECART’s recommendation is to prioritize Scenario A as the immediate implementation route because it maximizes time to market, accelerates the recovery of in-house corrugating capacity, and establishes the technical backbone on which Triplex and AMR automation can later be integrated with minimal disruption to production.

2. Project Context

Project Context and Strategic Objective

PSC Visalia currently operates as a converting-focused plant without the full economic benefit of integrated corrugating capacity under the scope of this project. The business rationale for the relocation is clear: bringing corrugating capability into the site reduces structural dependency on externally supplied board, improves lead-time control, increases production autonomy and creates a stronger manufacturing platform for future growth.

Based on the project discussions and the operating assumptions reviewed to date, the relocation of the BHS corrugator is the industrial catalyst that enables PSC to shift from a conversion-centric operating model to a more integrated corrugated packaging platform.

Strategic objectives of the project

  • Recover in-house corrugating capacity at PSC Visalia in the shortest practical time frame.
  • Reduce dependency on externally sourced corrugated board and the associated cost burden.
  • Improve production planning flexibility, service responsiveness and internal control of supply.
  • Design the installation so that future productivity modules can be integrated without major plant disruption.
  • Create a scalable base for future Triplex conversion and/or AMR intralogistics automation.
3. Commercial Positioning Logic

Recommended Project Logic: Time-to-Market First, Expansion by Phases

After the progressive refinement of the project scope, the recommended commercial and industrial logic is to separate the investment into a core implementation package and future productivity-expansion packages.

Core implementation package

The core package corresponds to the elements required to relocate, install and commission the BHS corrugator, together with the necessary peripherals and the local implementation costs to be assumed by PSC. This is the package that should be prioritized if the main objective is to bring the plant to the highest practical production capacity as fast as possible.

Future productivity-expansion packages

Triplex and AMR intralogistics remain highly valuable industrial levers, but they are not prerequisites for achieving the first milestone of this project: putting the corrugator into operation and stabilizing the integrated production flow. For that reason, both blocks are presented as future expansion scenarios that can be implemented once the base operation is running.

  • Triplex adds commercial flexibility, product range expansion and future portfolio differentiation.
  • AMR intralogistics acts as the structural enabler for WIP orchestration, material “in/out” traceability, waste-management discipline and the progressive elimination of non-value-added internal movements.
4. Why the Commercial Figures Evolved

Executive Clarification on Scope Refinement and Cost Evolution

During the course of the technical and commercial analysis, the project scope evolved from an initial relocation concept into a more precise industrial implementation plan. The final commercial figures therefore reflect not a correction of intent, but a more mature definition of the actual execution model required to deliver the project successfully in PSC Visalia.

Main reasons for the refinement of the project value

1) Confirmation of the actual machine origin and relocation route

Earlier working assumptions were based on a relocation reference associated with a BHS line previously considered under a different origin scenario. Once the project route and actual machine relocation basis were clarified, the machine relocation package was updated to reflect the correct dismantling, transport, packaging, shipping, installation and associated execution costs.

2) Formal integration of on-site project management into the execution model

As the implementation logic matured, it became necessary to explicitly include the on-site project management effort required to coordinate an installation of this complexity over the expected execution period. This is not an optional overhead but a key delivery layer that supports schedule control, technical coordination, subcontractor alignment, commissioning follow-up and site execution discipline.

3) Better visibility on transport, packaging and installation-related support activities

The final project structure now separates the core equipment value from the real logistics and implementation support required to execute the transfer safely and efficiently. Items such as packaging, transport, service cranes, extra shipping, local handling and installation support are therefore represented with greater accuracy than in early-stage working estimates.

4) Reassessment of import fees and customs-related cost exposure

Import fees were recalculated to better reflect the actual customs exposure of the project. For this business case, the fee estimate has been modeled on the basis that 65% of the imported project value corresponds to materials and equipment subject to customs release and associated import charges. This produces a more realistic picture of the local cost burden to be assumed by PSC under each scenario.

5) Separation between “must-have for start-up” and “high-value future productivity layers”

The project was also reorganized commercially to distinguish between the elements required to start production quickly and the additional automation layers that further optimize the plant once the base corrugating operation is stabilized. This is why Triplex and AMR intralogistics are now presented as structured future scenarios rather than being forced into the immediate time-to-market package.

Commercial interpretation. The current proposal should therefore be understood as the result of a more detailed engineering and execution review of the project, with the objective of giving PSC a more reliable basis for investment approval, implementation planning and scenario comparison.

5. Scenario Structure

Investment Scenarios Considered

Scenario A — BHS Corrugator Relocation Core Package

Scenario A is the recommended implementation route for immediate execution. It is the strongest platform for long-term operational efficiency, but it can be phased after the corrugator start-up to protect time to market.

Why AMR is the structural automation backbone

While Triplex extends product capability, AMR intralogistics extends operational control. It is the true cross-functional backbone of future automation because it connects material flow, WIP discipline, traceability and waste-management logic across the plant.

Value delivered by AMR intralogistics

  • Structured WIP movement between process islands and converting areas.
  • Reduction of non-value-added forklift movements and manual internal transport.
  • Improved material traceability for “in” and “out” flows.
  • Better process discipline in internal material replenishment and evacuation.
  • Platform for future waste collection, weighing and routing logic using AMRs and dedicated metallic cages or mobile containers.

Optional waste-management extension using the same AMR fleet

As an optional future layer, the same AMR fleet can be used to support internal waste-management flows from converting and corrugator workstations. Under this concept, metallic cages or dedicated containers can be transported by AMRs, weighed, and positioned where required, enabling more structured collection of trim and scrap while preserving a single intralogistics architecture.

Maintenance savings logic in Scenario C

Scenario C also creates a future opportunity for maintenance savings because it reduces dependence on the most maintenance-intensive internal handling assets. As forklift movements are progressively displaced and some conventional transfer equipment or conveyor dependency can be reduced, PSC can expect a lower maintenance burden associated with:

  • forklift fleets and related wear items,
  • high-frequency mechanical transport devices,
  • manual handling damage and congestion-related incidents,
  • indirect maintenance and supervision effort associated with fragmented internal flow systems.

INGECART recommends treating this maintenance benefit as a real but secondary upside to the main AMR business case. The primary value of the AMR package remains operational control, traceability, internal flow stability and the creation of a future-ready automation architecture.

10. ROI View

Economic Interpretation and ROI Logic

At this stage, the project should not be reduced to a single simplistic ROI figure. The economic logic is better understood through layers of value:

Layer 1 — Core value common to all scenarios

  • Recovery of in-house corrugating capacity.
  • Reduction of external board dependency.
  • Improved production autonomy and responsiveness.

Layer 2 — Scenario B upside

  • Commercial upside from expanded product capability.
  • Potential access to additional packaging structures and customer requirements.

Layer 3 — Scenario C upside

  • Reduced internal transport inefficiencies.
  • Higher traceability and WIP control.
  • Lower waste-handling disorder and improved material discipline.
  • Potential maintenance savings from lower dependence on forklifts and conventional transport assets.

Because the immediate strategic priority is speed of implementation, INGECART’s recommendation is to approve the project first through the time-to-market lens, and then use the stabilized operation to validate the additional ROI layers of Triplex and AMR through real production data.

11. INGECART Recommendation to PSC

INGECART Recommendation to PSC

Based on the current technical and commercial review, INGECART recommends PSC to structure the investment decision in phases rather than force all possible value layers into a single first-step package.

Recommendation 1 — Approve Scenario A as the immediate execution route

Scenario A is the most robust route to recover corrugating capacity quickly, establish production autonomy and bring the Visalia site into operation under a controlled implementation model.

Recommendation 2 — Keep Triplex as a second-stage strategic decision

Triplex remains a valuable option for future product and market expansion, but its greatest value will be captured once the corrugator base operation is already stabilized and PSC can connect the investment to a clearer commercial roadmap.

Recommendation 3 — Treat AMR intralogistics as the long-term operational backbone

AMR intralogistics should be considered the structural automation layer for future plant efficiency. It is the strongest platform for WIP orchestration, material traceability and future waste-management discipline, and it can be integrated in a later phase with lower disruption once the core corrugating operation is fully active.

Final positioning statement. INGECART therefore recommends a phased industrial roadmap: relocate and start the corrugator first, stabilize the production base, and then deploy the automation and product-expansion layers that maximize long-term plant performance.
