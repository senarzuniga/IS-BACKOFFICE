Executive Summary

This document analyzes the potential acquisition of the technological and industrial assets of Agil Packaging System S.L., currently undergoing insolvency proceedings. The objective is to evaluate the assets, the technology readiness level, the market potential, the industrial and commercial risks, the business generation scenarios, and the potential value creation pathways for an investor.

This document does not constitute a financial valuation of the company nor an investment recommendation, but rather a technical and strategic analysis based on the available information.

Key technical premises: The technology is designed to be integrated into existing assembly lines by adding a new processing station, without requiring new production plants. The system generates a thermoformed bubble base composed of 2 pieces for each under-counter appliance (washing machines, dishwashers, ovens, etc.).

2. Company situation

Corporate status

  • Company in insolvency proceedings.
  • Potential acquisition of the productive unit.
  • Known offer submitted by Fagor amounting to approximately €100,000 (based on available information).

Business activity

Development of an industrial solution aimed at replacing EPS (expanded polystyrene) based protection systems for household appliances with a "just-in-time" inflated technical film system.

The solution is integrated into existing assembly lines by adding a new inflation and sealing station, without the need to build new plants. Each under-counter appliance (washing machine, dishwasher, oven) receives a base composed of 2 thermoformed bubble pieces, generated from the technical film.

3. Assets to be acquired

Industrial assets

  • Process line (auxiliary elements from MTorres) for film handling.
  • APS inflation and laser sealing system (core of the system).
  • Auxiliary equipment · tools · testing facilities.

Technological assets and differential know-how

The core of the technology does not lie in the unwinders or winders (supplied by MTorres), but in the two-layer film bonding system, designed entirely by Agil. This system generates a spool ready for custom laser cutting and subsequent inflation, creating the thermoformed bubble geometry.

  • Two-layer bonding know-how: innovative, with potential application in other industrial sectors beyond appliance packaging.
  • Process parameters (temperature, pressure, timing).
  • Software for controlling bubble geometry.
  • Sealing patterns and validation procedures.

Intellectual property

The technological advantage relies on industrial know-how and accumulated development. The layer bonding process and the controlled inflation system constitute the main intangible asset.

A specific due diligence will be required on:

  • Potential registered patents (especially regarding the bonding method).
  • Trademarks and software.
  • Confidentiality agreements and effective ownership of developments.

Annex A · Industrial and technological assets

A.1 Industrial assets

The company owns a set of industrial assets that allow activity to be reactivated without having to redevelop the technology from scratch.

Two-layer film bonding system (technological core)

Equipment designed by Agil that constitutes the core of the technology. It allows the precise bonding of two film layers, generating a spool ready for custom laser cutting and sealing.

Main functions:

  • Synchronized feeding of the two film layers.
  • Controlled bonding via temperature and pressure (proprietary parameters).
  • Generation of the predefined bubble geometry.
  • Preparation of the welding pattern for laser cutting.

APS inflation and laser sealing system

Second industrial piece of equipment responsible for:

  • Controlled inflation of the bubbles.
  • Custom laser sealing.
  • Generation of the final thermoformed base (2 pieces).
  • "Just-in-time" manufacturing integrated into the assembly line.

Differential industrial know-how

Beyond the physical equipment, the company's main asset lies in:

  • Two-layer film bonding parameters.
  • Welding processes and inflation sequences.
  • Air chamber (bubble) geometries.
  • Mechanical performance of the thermoformed base.

Much of this knowledge constitutes industrial trade secrets accumulated over several years of development and has potential applications in other sectors.

Design tools

The company also has:

  • CAD designs of systems and tooling.
  • Manufacturing and control programs.
  • Testing procedures and validation records.

A.2 Technology readiness level

The technology is at an advanced stage. Its maturity can be considered close to TRL 8-9, as evidenced by:

  • Functional industrial equipment (full-scale prototypes).
  • Manufactured and tested parts.
  • Client validations (Mabe, Vestel).
  • Industrial demonstrations.

However, large-scale commercial deployment has not yet been achieved.

Annex C · Technological comparison

Conventional system (EPS)

The current standard process consists of:

EPS manufacturing → Transport → Storage → Deliver to line → Packaging

Main limitations:

  • Large storage volume (space occupation).
  • High logistics costs.
  • Low flexibility (format changes are costly).
  • Need for specific molds.

APS System (Agil)

The APS system completely modifies the flow, integrating into the existing line:

Film spool → 2-layer bonding → Laser cutting/sealing → Inflation → Thermoformed base (2 pieces)

Competitive advantages:

  • Elimination of the EPS warehouse.
  • Reduction of plant footprint.
  • Just-in-time manufacturing (on demand).
  • Automatic changeover between references (flexibility).
  • Proprietary know-how in two-layer film bonding, applicable to other sectors.

Annex D · Mabe case study

D.1 Current situation

Mabe has technically validated the bottom bases (2-piece thermoformed bubble). A pre-series of approximately 100 units is currently planned.

The objective is to validate:

  • Mechanical performance and resistance.
  • Assembly and line integration process.
  • Logistics and handling.

D.2 Possible industrialization

In case of positive validation, implementation would require:

  • Installation of the bonding and inflation station in the existing line.
  • Local supply of technical film.
  • Staff training.

D.3 Supply chain

Currently, molds and tooling are supplied by specialized manufacturers.

  • Estimated cost of tooling: €7,000 per reference.
  • Estimated unit cost of bases (industrialized): less than €2 per piece.

D.4 Market potential

For a plant producing 1 million washing machines/year, approximately 2 million pieces would be required (1 base = 2 pieces).

With estimated market prices between €2.5 and €4 per base (set of 2 pieces), the annual economic volume associated solely with the bases could range between €5 and €8 million/year.

Recurring technical film consumption would need to be added to this.

Annex E · Business model

The technology allows for different exploitation schemes.

System sales

  • High initial revenue (bonding + inflation equipment).
  • Lower recurrence.

Leasing / Renting

  • Lower initial investment for the client.
  • Higher recurrence and customer loyalty.

Consumable sales (technical film)

  • Constitutes the main recurring revenue.
  • Greater stability and attractive margins at volume.
  • Generates client dependency on the film supplier.

Services and licensing

  • Maintenance and technical assistance.
  • Engineering for new formats and applications.
  • Licensing of the layer bonding know-how for other sectors (automotive, construction).

Annex F · Main risks

Commercial risk

The main challenge identified is not technological. It is the speed of adoption by manufacturers accustomed to EPS.

Replacement implies modifications to:

  • Logistics and storage.
  • Assembly line automation.
  • Staff training.

Industrial risk

The technology has been validated at the prototype level. However, it will be necessary to:

  • Scale up production of film and components.
  • Ensure the repeatability of the bonding process at high speed.
  • Maintain consistent quality in different industrial environments.

Financial risk

The company will require sufficient financial capacity to:

  • Finance implementation in client plants.
  • Support long sales and validation cycles.
  • Develop new references and applications.

Market risk

Acceptance will depend on:

  • Regulatory evolution (EPS bans).
  • Price of recycled plastic versus virgin plastic.
  • Speed of ESG criteria implementation by manufacturers.

Annex G · Industrial partners and consortium structure

G.1 SPM Engineering s.r.l.

SPM Engineering s.r.l. is an international engineering and product design consultancy located in Fiume Veneto (Pordenone), northern Italy. The company has a team of between 30 and 50 specialized workers and stands out for its deep experience in the development, analysis, and validation of household appliances (white-goods), as well as complex systems for industrial processes and automotive.

Main characteristics:

  • Strategic location: Headquarters in the heart of the Italian industrial region for appliances.
  • Industrial focus: Experts in the design of household and industrial appliances (washing machines, dishwashers, ovens, refrigerators).
  • Comprehensive services: Cover everything from preliminary market analysis (benchmarking) to industrial modeling and component engineering.
  • Simulation infrastructure: They use over 20 advanced software tools for virtual simulations and structural and fluid performance calculations.
  • Testing laboratory: They have their own laboratory specialized in testing physical functional prototypes, ensuring compliance with international robustness and energy efficiency standards.
  • Innovation projects: They develop their own patents aimed at optimizing domestic and industrial processes.
🤝 Interest expressed – SPM Engineering has shown its willingness to participate in a potential consortium for the acquisition and industrialization of Agil's technology, contributing specialized technical knowledge and key contacts in the appliance sector, especially in the European market (Italy, Germany, Turkey).

G.2 Interest of current Agilpack partners

The current partners of Agilpack have expressed their willingness to collaborate economically in the project to acquire and reactivate the company, contributing capital and operational expertise.

🔒 CONFIDENTIAL – LEGAL RESTRICTION
This information must not appear in the public version of the document nor be communicated externally. Since Agilpack is in insolvency proceedings, the direct participation of the current partners in the purchase of assets could be interpreted as a self-dealing operation at a low price, which would generate legal risks and potential challenges from the insolvency administration and other creditors.

Implication for the investor: This interest demonstrates the partners' commitment and confidence in the project. However, it must be legally structured (for example, through an independent investment vehicle or through capital injection after the acquisition) to avoid any conflict of interest or violation of insolvency regulations. The legal due diligence must analyze this aspect in depth.
