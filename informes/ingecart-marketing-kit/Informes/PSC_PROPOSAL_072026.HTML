1. Executive Summary

Pacific Southwest Container (PSC) is not simply evaluating the relocation of a corrugator. The decision at Visalia is fundamentally a choice between two different industrial models for capturing value from in-house corrugating capacity.

Scenario A is a classic equipment relocation project: move the BHS corrugator to Visalia, install the required peripherals and optionally add a Triplex configuration to expand product capability.

Scenario B uses the same corrugator relocation as the foundation of a broader industrial redesign: automated reel feeding with INGETRANS, structured AMR intralogistics for WIP management, optional waste collection logic using the same AMR fleet, higher traceability, better material flow and lower operating friction between corrugating and converting.

The strategic implication is clear: Scenario A solves the need for in-house corrugating, while Scenario B transforms the Visalia site into a more integrated, traceable and scalable manufacturing platform.

Main preliminary conclusions

  • Both scenarios are justified by the elimination of external board sourcing and associated logistics, estimated at approximately $2.5M–$3.0M per year.
  • Scenario A is the lower-CAPEX, lower-complexity route and adds the optional Triplex capability for future product diversification.
  • Scenario B requires an estimated additional $1.229M of CAPEX versus Scenario A, but creates structural value beyond board insourcing through automation, WIP flow control, reduced handling losses, lower maintenance burden on conventional internal transport assets and stronger operating scalability.
  • Under a realistic industrial case, the additional CAPEX of Scenario B can be justified by incremental savings in labour, maintenance, handling losses, forklift dependence and productivity stability, on top of the common board-sourcing savings shared by both scenarios.

2. Project Context and Decision Framework

2.1 Current position of PSC Visalia

PSC Visalia operates as a converting-oriented plant with strong box manufacturing capability and significant site scale, but without the same level of vertically integrated corrugating autonomy that would allow it to fully internalize board production for local converting demand.

Based on the information available for PSC’s manufacturing footprint and the working assumptions shared for this project, Visalia is considered a mid-sized site within the PSC network, with an estimated annual revenue in the range of $50M–$80M. For the purposes of this business case, the economic modelling is anchored on a $50M annual revenue base to maintain a conservative ROI view.

2.2 Main structural inefficiency today

The current operating model depends on external board supply or board transfer from outside the site. This creates a recurring structural cost burden and operational constraints:

  • Recurring logistics cost for corrugated board supply into Visalia
  • Lower flexibility to react to changes in converting demand
  • Longer planning loops between board availability and converting schedules
  • Lower visibility over true paper consumption, internal losses and order-level material traceability
  • Reduced ability to optimize the physical relationship between corrugating, WIP and converting flows

2.3 Core economic driver

The project is primarily supported by the elimination of the current external board sourcing and transport burden, estimated at:

Base savings driver Estimated annual value Comment
External board sourcing / transfer cost eliminated $2,500,000 – $3,000,000 / year Common savings layer for both Scenario A and Scenario B

This is the base ROI layer for the entire project. The role of the business case is therefore not to justify corrugator relocation itself — that part is already strongly supported — but to determine whether PSC should stop at the equipment level or use the project to redesign the industrial flow of the site.

3. Strategic Objective of the Investment

The investment should be assessed against six strategic goals:

  • Bring corrugating capacity into the Visalia plant.
  • Reduce dependency on external board supply and transfer logistics.
  • Improve responsiveness to converting demand and customer service requirements.
  • Integrate corrugating and converting under one site-level production logic.
  • Increase material traceability, process visibility and control of real paper consumption.
  • Determine whether the project should be limited to “installing a corrugator” or used to create a higher-performance future operating model for the plant.

4. Definition of the Two Scenarios

4.1 Scenario A – Corrugator relocation + peripherals + Triplex option

Scenario A is a relocation-led investment model. It focuses on moving the BHS corrugator to PSC Visalia, installing the required auxiliary systems and adding the Triplex configuration as a route to future product capability expansion.

Included scope

  • BHS corrugator relocation, transport, disassembly, installation, upgrades and commissioning
  • Project peripherals required for operation
  • Triplex package
  • PSC local items: boiler, civil work, transport, packaging, crane rental and fees

4.2 Scenario B – Industrial redesign + corrugator + INGETRANS + AMR intralogistics

Scenario B uses the same corrugator relocation as the technical base, but changes the project logic from a machine move to an integrated industrial transformation. It combines corrugating capacity with automated reel feeding, structured WIP intralogistics and expanded traceability of material flow.

Included scope

  • BHS corrugator relocation, transport, disassembly, installation, upgrades and commissioning
  • Project peripherals required for operation
  • INGETRANS automated reel feeding and reel flow management
  • AMR intralogistics for WIP movements between corrugating and converting islands
  • Material traceability improvement for “in” and “out” flow between production stages
  • Optional future extension: internal waste collection and management using the same AMR fleet with metal cages, weighing logic and controlled drop-off points
  • PSC local items: boiler, civil work, transport, packaging, crane rental and fees

Interpretation of the CAPEX gap

The updated cost structure changes the earlier narrative materially. Scenario B is not cheaper than Scenario A; it is approximately $1.23M higher in upfront CAPEX. However, the relevant management question is not whether Scenario B is more expensive at purchase level, but whether that additional investment creates a superior industrial and financial result over the life of the asset.

6. Industrial Value of Scenario A

6.1 What Scenario A solves

Scenario A addresses the core need of bringing corrugating in-house and removing the external board dependency. This alone can be enough to support the investment case if PSC’s objective is primarily to recover board autonomy with a contained execution scope.

6.2 Main benefits of Scenario A

  • Elimination of external board supply cost
  • Greater autonomy over corrugating capacity
  • Improved responsiveness to converting demand
  • Better integration of board production and box conversion at site level
  • Potential margin improvement from internalized corrugating

6.3 Strategic value of the Triplex package

The Triplex package gives PSC an option to expand its product portfolio and potentially address higher-value structures or new customer applications. However, in the current business case, the Triplex upside should be treated as commercial potential not yet quantified, unless PSC validates a concrete sales case with product mix, volumes and margin assumptions.

6.4 Limitations of Scenario A

  • The project remains primarily a machine installation and capacity recovery exercise.
  • It does not structurally redesign the internal relationship between corrugating, WIP and converting.
  • It does not automate reel feeding to the corrugator.
  • It does not create the same level of traceability over actual reel consumption and returns.
  • It does not structurally address internal handling losses, forklift dependency or the operating friction of WIP movements.

7. Industrial Value of Scenario B

7.1 Strategic positioning of Scenario B

Scenario B should not be read as “Scenario A plus some automation”. It is a different operating model. The purpose is to use the corrugator relocation as the trigger to redesign the physical and digital flow of the plant around three priorities:

  • Reliable reel supply to the corrugator
  • Controlled WIP flow between corrugating and converting islands
  • Higher traceability and lower operating friction across internal material movements

7.2 Value created by INGETRANS automated reel feeding

  • Automates reel supply to the corrugator and reduces manual intervention around the wet end.
  • Improves reel availability and reduces the risk of production interruptions caused by reel logistics.
  • Provides a better basis for reel traceability, consumed material tracking and return handling.
  • Improves safety and ergonomics by reducing forklift traffic and operator intervention near the corrugator.
  • Supports more stable corrugator operation and lower variability in reel handling performance.

7.3 Value created by AMR intralogistics for WIP

1. Structured internal flow between corrugating and converting

The AMR intralogistics layer introduces a controlled and repeatable transport logic for intermediate loads moving from corrugating to converting islands and between downstream process points. Instead of relying on conventional ad-hoc forklift movements, the plant can establish a more disciplined flow architecture for WIP, with defined pick-up, delivery and replenishment rules.

  • Reduction of non-productive internal transport movements
  • Better synchronization between corrugating output and converting demand
  • Improved service to converting lines with less dependence on opportunistic forklift availability
  • Higher order and flow discipline in the plant
  • Better scalability of internal logistics as volume grows

2. Additional value from AMR intralogistics for WIP management

Beyond pure transport automation, the AMR layer creates value by reducing the need for conventional internal handling assets and by improving control of material movements between production stages.

  • Reduction of forklift intensity for repetitive WIP moves
  • Lower reliance on transfer conveyors and auxiliary transport elements for load handling
  • Improved traceability of “material in” and “material out” between process islands
  • Potential reduction of handling-related damage, mix-ups and misplaced loads
  • Better visibility over where intermediate loads are, where they are going and which production order they belong to

Optional extension not valued in the current CAPEX case: the same AMR fleet can also be configured to support internal waste collection from corrugator and converting workstations by using metal cages as mobile containers. In this optional configuration, AMRs would transport the cages, route them to weighing points, and reposition them at designated accumulation or discharge areas. This creates a practical foundation for future waste traceability, controlled scrap evacuation and more accurate measurement of handling losses without requiring a dedicated second transport fleet.

7.4 Additional maintenance savings potential in Scenario B

A relevant value lever that was not explicitly quantified in the original version of the report is the maintenance savings associated with replacing part of the conventional internal logistics infrastructure.

Under a traditional corrugator-to-converting logistics model, the site typically depends on a combination of:

  • Forklifts and related batteries, tires, hydraulic components and service hours
  • Transfer conveyors and short transport sections with frequent wear items
  • Additional handling interfaces, sensors, mechanical stops and accumulation points that require regular intervention

Scenario B reduces a meaningful share of this maintenance burden by shifting repetitive internal transport tasks toward AMRs and by reducing the need for some conventional conveyor infrastructure that would otherwise be required for load handling.

Maintenance savings lever in Scenario B Expected effect
Lower forklift fleet intensity for repetitive internal transport Reduced spend on servicing, tires, batteries, forks, hydraulics, breakdown interventions and operator-related damage to transport assets
Lower dependence on transfer conveyors and mechanical load-handling interfaces Reduced wear-part replacement, alignment work, motor/drive maintenance and unplanned stoppages associated with conventional transport systems
Fewer handling interfaces and cleaner transport logic Lower maintenance overhead on sensors, stops, transfer points and congestion-related damage
Improved flow predictability Lower emergency maintenance intensity caused by rushed handling, collisions or non-standard transport practices

At this stage, INGECART recommends treating this maintenance benefit as a real but not yet fully valued savings block unless PSC shares its current annual maintenance cost for forklifts, transfer conveyors and related internal transport equipment. Once that data is available, the business case can incorporate a quantified annual maintenance reduction for Scenario B.

7.5 Waste reduction and traceability logic in Scenario B

Scenario B also creates the operational conditions to reduce handling-related losses and improve the traceability of material loss. This value comes from three mechanisms:

  • Better control of reel feeding and reel consumption through INGETRANS
  • Reduced internal damage, confusion and misrouting of WIP loads through controlled AMR flows
  • Optional future integration of structured waste collection and weighing logic using the AMR fleet

Using the conservative assumption already discussed for PSC Visalia, if total waste-related economic leakage is approximated at around 1% of annual plant revenue on a $50M site, the order of magnitude of that loss is approximately $500,000 per year. Scenario B does not eliminate that full amount, but it gives PSC a much stronger platform to capture a meaningful share of it through better handling discipline, improved traceability and tighter material control.

11. INGECART Recommendations to PSC

11.1 Recommendation on how PSC should frame the decision

INGECART recommends that PSC does not evaluate this project purely as a comparison of machine packages or initial CAPEX. The decision should be framed as a choice between:

  • Scenario A: a corrugating capacity recovery project with optional product extension through Triplex
  • Scenario B: a broader industrial redesign that uses the corrugator move to improve internal logistics, traceability, operating stability and long-term scalability

11.2 Recommendation if PSC prioritizes lower CAPEX and simpler execution

If PSC’s main objective is to internalize corrugating capacity as quickly as possible with the most contained investment scope, Scenario A is a valid and defendable path. It captures the base board-sourcing savings and offers the possibility of future Triplex-driven commercial upside.

11.3 Recommendation if PSC wants to maximize long-term industrial value

If PSC wants to use this project to create a more competitive operating model for Visalia, INGECART recommends Scenario B as the stronger long-term industrial solution, provided PSC is willing to support the additional CAPEX and validate the operating savings with plant data.

The rationale is that Scenario B addresses not only where the corrugator sits, but also how material flows through the plant, how WIP is moved, how reel supply is controlled, and how future traceability and waste-management logic can be built on the same infrastructure.

11.4 Recommended phased implementation logic

To reduce execution risk and avoid an “all-or-nothing” decision, INGECART recommends that PSC consider Scenario B as a phased industrial roadmap:

Recommended phase Scope Purpose
Phase 1 BHS corrugator relocation + peripherals + correct layout definition + INGETRANS Secure corrugating capacity, create the right physical architecture and stabilize reel logistics from day one
Phase 2 AMR intralogistics for WIP between corrugating and converting islands Reduce forklift dependence, structure internal flow and capture logistics and maintenance savings
Phase 3 Optional AMR-based waste collection and traceability logic Improve waste visibility, internal scrap handling discipline and measurement of material loss
Phase 4 Triplex evaluation once the base flow is stabilized Assess product portfolio expansion on top of a mature operating platform

11.5 Data requested by INGECART to close the final business case

To convert this executive case into a fully validated investment model, INGECART recommends that PSC share the following plant-specific information:

  • Current annual external board supply / transfer cost by source and route
  • Current forklift fleet size, utilization and annual maintenance cost
  • Current transfer conveyor / auxiliary transport maintenance cost
  • Labour structure for internal WIP movements and indirect logistics tasks
  • Waste / trim / scrap metrics by corrugator and converting area
  • Current service levels, bottlenecks and WIP congestion points between corrugating and converting

12. Final Management View

The base project case is already strong because both alternatives eliminate a recurring board-sourcing burden that appears to be in the order of $2.5M–$3.0M per year. The real management decision is therefore whether PSC wants to stop at board insourcing or use the same investment window to create a better future plant architecture.

Scenario A is the right answer if PSC wants the most direct path to in-house corrugating capacity with a lower initial investment and a possible Triplex commercial upside.

Scenario B is the stronger answer if PSC wants to use this project to redesign how the plant works: more reliable reel logistics, more structured WIP flow, lower dependence on conventional internal transport, better traceability and a more scalable industrial platform.

INGECART’s view is that the final recommendation should be based on PSC’s appetite for operational transformation. If the objective is only to “install a corrugator”, Scenario A is sufficient. If the objective is to improve the economics and operating discipline of the Visalia site for the next decade, Scenario B deserves serious consideration and should be validated with plant data at the next stage.
